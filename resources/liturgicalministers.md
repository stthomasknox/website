# Liturgical Minister Training
_prepared by Fr. Jordan C. Fetcko_

## General Principles

### Reverence
- Reverence is an attitude of respect for God and the sacred
- Reverence is primarily interior but is necessarily expressed exteriorly
- Interior and exterior reverence feed and strengthen each other

### Genuflection and Bowing
> The gestures and bodily posture of both the Priest, the Deacon, and the ministers, and also of the people, must be conducive to making the entire celebration resplendent with beauty and noble simplicity, to making clear the true and full meaning of its different parts, and to fostering the participation of all. Attention must therefore be paid to what is determined by this General Instruction and by the traditional practice of the Roman Rite and to what serves the common spiritual good of the People of God, rather than private inclination or arbitrary choice.
> 
> A common bodily posture, to be observed by all those taking part, is a sign of the unity of the members of the Christian community gathered together for the Sacred Liturgy, for it expresses the intentions and spiritual attitude of the participants and also fosters them. (_General Instruction of the Roman Missal_ \[_GIRM_], no. 42)
- Outside of Mass: genuflect (“by bending the right knee to the ground” \[_GIRM,_ no. 274]) to the tabernacle after entering and before leaving a sacred space where the Blessed Sacrament is reserved; genuflect to the tabernacle before entering (and after leaving) the sanctuary and when passing directly in front of the tabernacle itself
- During Mass: make a profound bow (a bow at the waist) toward the altar before entering and after leaving the sanctuary and whenever passing directly in front of the altar itself
- A bow of the head is made at the names of the three Persons of the Trinity named together, the Names of Jesus and of Mary, and the name of any Saint(s) celebrated on that day (no. 275); a bow of the head is also customary before receiving Communion in the United States (no. 160)

### Prayerful Silence
- The nave and sanctuary of a church building are primarily places of prayer, and this reality is acknowledged and respected by keeping an attitude of prayerful silence while in these spaces
- Certainly, speech is not absolutely forbidden there, but what you say in sacred spaces should be important, brief, and spoken quietly and at an appropriate moment
- The Roman Missal calls it “praiseworthy” to extend this practice to the sacristy as well (cf. _GIRM,_ no. 45)

## Specific Guidance

### Lectors
- Your role as lectors is, first and foremost, to have a living love of the Word of God, Who is Jesus Christ; “ignorance of Scripture is ignorance of Christ” (St. Jerome), and to know Scripture is to know Christ
- **Never** proclaim a reading at Mass that you have not first at least read over; ideally, you should read over, pray with, and practice aloud the reading(s) assigned to you
- The microphone is meant to assist you rather than be used as a “crutch” that compensates for quiet or unclear speech; practice a loud and clear public speaking voice which is powerful without sounding like shouting
- Read the opening (“A reading from…”), the closing (“The Word of the Lord”), and everything in between as it is written in the Lectionary; on-the-fly modifications are not tolerated
- If there is no music minister at Mass, read the Responsorial Psalm after the First Reading: first say the antiphon, without introduction, and allow the assembly to repeat it after you; there is no need to raise one or both arms to signal the appropriate moment for the response
- Pronunciation guides for names found in the Lectionary can be consulted in the sacristy
- After you have finished the Second Reading, note whether the Book of the Gospels is on the altar or not: if it is, then close the Lectionary and place it on the shelf, otherwise leave it open and in place for the Gospel Reading

### Music Ministers
- The task of cantors/choir members is to lead and support the singing at Mass, not to perform; any appearance of the latter is to be avoided
- When chanting the Responsorial Psalm, there is no need to raise one or both arms to signal the assembly to join in singing the antiphon; the musical setting already communicates this
- If you are standing during the Eucharistic Prayer due to the demands of your ministry, make a profound bow toward the altar as the priest celebrant genuflects after the elevation of (what is now) the Body of Christ and the chalice containing the Blood of Christ (cf. _GIRM,_ no. 43)

### Extraordinary Ministers of Holy Communion
- There is one non-negotiable for EMHCs: to have a Catholic faith in the Real Presence of Jesus Christ in the Eucharist; no one lacking this faith is fit to serve as EMHC, and everything you do as an EMHC flows from this belief
- Do everything you reasonably can to ensure that the host—or even the tiniest fragments of the host—does not fall to the floor, nor that even a drop of Precious Blood is spilled; this is achieved by, for example, placing the host directly on the hand or tongue of the communicant (rather than dropping it onto either), and by always holding the ciborium or chalice with both hands whenever possible (especially while walking)
- If a host falls to the floor, pick it up immediately and, if you are comfortable doing so, consume it yourself; if you are not comfortable doing so, place the fallen host into the _ablution cup_ (small lidded cup of water) by the tabernacle and inform the celebrant after Communion or after Mass
- If any of the Precious Blood is spilled, immediately place your purificator over the affected area and inform the celebrant as soon as feasible
- We must take the risk of profanation very seriously: if you see anyone walking away without consuming the Body of Christ, you *must* pursue them and instruct them to consume it
- If you are holding the very Body and Blood of Christ, do not make any gesture of reverence toward the altar, etc.: why bow to the altar when Jesus Himself is in your hands?

### Ushers
- Your movement around the church, though at times necessary, should be as unobtrusive as possible, so as not to draw people’s attention away from the Eucharist
- After the collection is taken up and consolidated into a single basket, one usher should process down the center aisle with the basket; when he reaches the main altar, he should pause, make a bow of the head, and then proceed toward St. Joseph’s altar (to the right), placing the basket in front of—not on—that side altar
- If anything requiring prompt response transpires during the course of Mass—for example, someone passes out or makes a disturbance—you should be prepared to act, rather than waiting for the priest celebrant to react, respond, or direct
